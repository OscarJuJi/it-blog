## [Why UUIDv7 guarantees uniqueness but can break timestamp assumptions](https://alexsci.com/blog/uuidv7-is-weird/)

An analysis of RFC 9562 implementations reveals that UUIDv7 identifiers do not strictly guarantee accurate timestamps derived from the system clock. In Python's standard library implementation, high generation volume or backward clock shifts can force the embedded timestamp to advance ahead of real time without recovering. Working software engineers should care because UUIDv7 is frequently adopted under the false assumption that its embedded timestamp can safely replace separate creation-time database columns or primary index ordering. In practice, backend developers must continue maintaining explicit creation timestamp columns for application logic instead of decoding UUIDv7 bit fields. Engineering teams should also audit how their language runtimes handle counter overflows to prevent unbounded timestamp drift in distributed systems.
